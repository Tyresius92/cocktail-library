# Gin

Flavored vodka: a neutral grain spirit flavored with botanicals, most notably juniper (required to be called gin in the US and EU), plus coriander, orris root, angelica, citrus peel, star anise, licorice and more. Developed specifically to be mixed. Bright, alcoholic and forest-y on the nose; each brand steers a cocktail differently (juniper-heavy, high-proof Tanqueray is more assertive than citrusy Plymouth). The book picks specific brands for each cocktail because gin substitutions work worse than for other spirits; if you must substitute, stay in the same category: London dry or contemporary.

## Types
- **London dry**: the most pervasive style: clean, nearly neutral foundation with sharp, spicy, juniper-dominated flavor. Bottles: [[Beefeater London Dry gin]], [[Fords gin]], [[Plymouth gin]], [[Sipsmith gin]], [[Tanqueray gin]].
- **Old Tom**: once-defunct slightly sweet style, thought to predate London dry; used where a soft London dry (Plymouth, Fords) might be; the Martinez and Tom Collins were likely made with it. [[Hayman's Old Tom gin]]; Ransom Old Tom is lightly aged.
- **Contemporary**: avant-garde gins that eschew conventions; devise cocktails by working from the gin backward. [[Aviation gin]], [[St. George Botanivore gin]], [[St. George Terroir gin]], [[St. George Dry Rye gin]].

## Gin and proof
Proof varies by market, largely because of taxes (Beefeater is 47% in the US, 40% in the UK). Check the label: higher-proof gins are spicier and more assertive (pull back on the measure), lower-proof gins are often more citrusy.

## Aquavit
Scandinavian, powerfully flavored, clear (lightly aged); dominated by caraway or anise rather than juniper, spicy and savory. Can overwhelm, so use in collaboration with other spirits. Favorites: Linie (Norway) and Krogstad (Oregon).
