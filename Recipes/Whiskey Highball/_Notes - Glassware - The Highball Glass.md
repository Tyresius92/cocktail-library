# Glassware: The Highball Glass (p215, condensed)

Highballs are all about the bubbles, so use a glass that keeps effervescence alive. A short, wide glass (Old-Fashioned or coupe) exposes more surface area, so gases leave faster than from a tall, narrow glass.

- The authors' ideal Highball glass holds 12 oz, with a thick weighted base and sides that thin out at the rim (solid footing, delicate rim; thin rims chip). Etching is mostly ornamental but adds grip. Many favorites come from Japan; the favorite Stateside brand is Hard Strong, which makes durable Highball glasses.
- Highball glasses and Collins glasses are not interchangeable: a proper Collins holds about 14 oz. A Highball is 2 oz booze plus mixer, while a Collins is a full shaken cocktail over ice topped with bubbles. A Collins glass is fine for Highballs, but a Collins-style drink in a Highball glass means less mixer or fewer ice cubes. Same for swizzles and coolers.

## Waste Not (sidebar)
Most bars serve Highballs with a plastic straw; the authors phased them out for environmental reasons. Most cocktails don't need straws. They provide them on request and use them only for drinks that need them (Juleps, swizzles, crushed-ice drinks), and use reusable metal straws.
