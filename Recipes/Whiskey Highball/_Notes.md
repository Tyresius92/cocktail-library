# The Whisky Highball (Chapter 5, pp. 199-)

Root recipes: [[Whisky Highball]] (classic, p199) and [[Ideal Whisky Highball]] (the book's version, p200).

## Whisky Highball Orthodoxy (p201)
- A Highball is composed of a core spirit that also provides seasoning, and is balanced by a nonalcoholic mixer.
- A Highball's core can be split between any number of spirits, wines or fortified wines.
- A Highball can be effervescent or still.

## Devil in the Details (p201, condensed)
On paper just whisky and carbonated water, like other rail drinks (vodka soda, gin and tonic, rum and Coke). The authors see mastering the Highball as a key accomplishment for any bartender: it speaks volumes about knowledge, preparation and technique. In Tokyo's Ginza bars, the few elements are each carefully considered: the whisky's character; the size, shape and clarity of the ice; the temperature, effervescence and mineral content of the water; the heft of the glass and thickness of its rim; and the meticulous way it's assembled and served. Making a great Highball requires mastering both ingredients and tools. See also "Exploring Technique: Building Whisky Highballs" (p214).

## Understanding the Template (p201-202, condensed)
Success depends on ingredients that work well together (flavor affinities like gin and tonic; how a sparkling water's minerals interact with a scotch's brininess). The Highball is a simple but extremely flexible template: a spirit plus a mixer. Any spirit can be the core; mixers include seltzer, tonic, ginger ale or beer, colas and other sodas, even juices (a Mimosa is a Champagne and orange juice Highball; a Greyhound is gin and grapefruit juice; the Bloody Mary is a savory Highball). Possibilities expand by splitting the core, the mixer, or both: the Americano (p208) splits Campari and sweet vermouth, topped with seltzer; the Aperol Spritz (p223) has Aperol as core and splits the mixer between seltzer and prosecco. Small amounts of other flavorings expand it further: the Harvey Wallbanger (vodka, orange juice, float of Galliano), the Tequila Sunrise (tequila, orange juice, grenadine).

Craft is careful study of ingredients and refined technique; art is applying creativity to those skills. The Highball is where everything in the book is fully articulated. The lines between a Highball and a Collins blur: the book draws it at 3/4 oz citrus juice; above that the drink is more closely affiliated with sours such as the Daiquiri. In a Highball, citrus and other acids accent other flavors rather than being central.

## The Core and Seasoning: Whisky (p202-)
A high-quality spirit is the basis, since the drink has little to hide behind, and because seltzer is neutral the whisky also serves as the main seasoning. The authors make most of their whiskey cocktails with American whiskey (so many classics use bourbon or rye, and domestic is more affordable), but are using more international whiskies as they find affordable bottles. Whiskies all come from grains cooked into a mash, then distilled and aged in oak; differences lie in which grains are used, how they're processed, and the distilling.
