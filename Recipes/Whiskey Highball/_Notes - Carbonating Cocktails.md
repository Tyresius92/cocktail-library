# Next-Level Technique: Carbonating Cocktails (pp228-232, condensed)

Why: CO2 bubbles lift aromatics, tickle the tongue and make acidity read as more refreshing. Carbonated drinks here: [[Nobody's Robots]], [[Normandie Club Spritz No. 1]], [[Normandie Club Spritz No. 3]].

## Three variables (p229)
- **Clarity**: particles (citrus pulp etc.) give bubbles an escape route, so use clear liquids; clarify first ([[_Notes - Clarifying Juice]], p144).
- **Temperature**: colder liquid holds more CO2; chill everything before and after carbonating.
- **Time**: even cold, clear liquid needs time to absorb the gas; finer, tighter bubbles after resting at least a day.

## When not to carbonate (p230)
Skip spirit-forward cocktails (e.g. a Negroni): carbonation amplifies aromatics, acidity and perceived alcohol and shifts the balance, and fizzy drinks get drunk faster. Favor lighter-proof drinks.

## Preparing ingredients (p230)
1. Filter and clarify cloudy juices, infusions and syrups.
2. Chill: spirits (40% ABV+) in the freezer at least 12 hours; lower-proof items (vermouth, wine, liqueurs) refrigerated, or in an ice bath for at least 1 hour (don't freeze); syrups and juices refrigerated 1-4 hours.
3. Prep the space and tools: clean rig, a chilled siphon canister if using one, scale, towel, measuring cup.
4. Have small ingredients (bitters, extracts, acid solutions) handy.
5. Dilute the batch with very cold seltzer so about 20 percent of total volume is water.
6. Measure each ingredient into the vessel quickly to keep everything cold, then carbonate.

## Methods (p230-232)
Three methods: soda siphon, DIY bottle rig (favorite), DIY keg rig. A Perlini system is another option. Don't use a SodaStream: it voids the warranty and gives no pressure control. See [[_Notes - Home Carbonation Rig]] for the bottle and keg steps.

**Soda siphon** (iSi favorite): fill no higher than the Max line, screw the lid on tight, charge with one CO2 cartridge and shake hard (they also charge a second time and shake again). Chill at least 2 hours, preferably 6. Drawbacks: can't purge air without wasting a charger, can't adjust pressure, and the cartridges are costly and wasteful. Good starter option.

## Carbonated Fruit (p231 sidebar)
Use an iSi whipper (not a soda siphon). CO2 passes through the fruit's skin and dissolves in its liquid, giving fizzy fruit as a garnish. Grapes work best (berries are too delicate; cut larger fruit, citrus segments into quarters). Fill to the Max line only, seal, charge with a cartridge while holding the valve open to purge air, then close the valve and charge again. Chill in an ice bath at least 1 hour; overnight gives very fizzy fruit. Whole grapes tend to burst, so halve them or add a complementary liquid such as [[_Notes - Verjus]] to buffer the skins. Fizzy fruit can feel "fermented" to the brain, so complementary liquids help keep it tasting fresh.
