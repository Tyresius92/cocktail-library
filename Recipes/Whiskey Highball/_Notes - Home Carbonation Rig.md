# Home Carbonation Rig: Bottles and Kegs (pp233-237, condensed)

Favorite method, popularized by Dave Arnold and Jeffrey Morgenthaler. Small upfront cost, then carbonating (or an endless supply of seltzer) is very cheap. Part of [[_Notes - Carbonating Cocktails]].

## What to buy (p233)
- CO2 tank: 5-lb (about 14 in tall) with standard fitting, or 20-lb for hundreds of carbonations; swap when empty.
- Primary CO2 regulator: pressure (PSI) and tank-level dials, shutoff valve, adjustable up to 60 PSI. Micro Matic preferred.
- Hoses: 5 feet of 5/16-inch ID gas hose; thick-walled braided tubing (Micro Matic) preferred over red flexible hose for the higher pressure (beer systems rarely exceed 20 PSI).
- Gas ball lock connector (hose to cap), at least two worm clips (buy extras), carbonation cap (one-way valve), plastic PET bottles 1-3 L (clean well if reused soda bottles).

## How to carbonate bottled liquids (p234)
1. Chill all ingredients.
2. Funnel into the bottle, no more than 4/5 full.
3. Squeeze out air, attach the carbonation cap snugly.
4. Gas on, regulator at **45 PSI**.
5. Attach the gas ball lock connector to the cap; the bottle expands within seconds.
6. Shake 10 seconds while attached, then disconnect the hose.
7. Refrigerate or ice bath at least 2 hours.
8. Optional second round: after 1 hour rest, squeeze out air, reseal, repeat steps 4-7, chill another hour.
9. Serve: hold upright, open the cap carefully (loud hiss), serve quickly.

## Kegs (pp236-237)
Same idea at larger scale; check local law on serving liquor from anything but its original bottle commercially. Don't retrofit a beer system (cocktails are more acidic and higher proof, and flavor the lines). For home use, a 2 1/2-gallon keg; for bars, 5-gallon.

**What to buy:** everything from the bottle rig except the cap and bottles, plus a ball lock cornelius keg (clean new gaskets, undented top; Torpedo kegs from Morebeer.com preferred), a carbonating lid (tube with carbonating stone), and a tap with ball lock liquid connector and simple faucet.

**How to carbonate a keg:**
1. Chill ingredients; clean keg inside and out and check O-rings.
2. Pour cold ingredients in, about half full to leave room for dilution.
3. Dilute with very cold seltzer last; never fill beyond 4/5.
4. Gas on, regulator at 45 PSI.
5. Attach the gas-to-gas connector in the middle of the carbonating lid.
6. Let gas disperse for 1 minute.
7. Pull the release valve about 2 seconds to swap oxygen for CO2.
8. If the line allows, lay the keg on its side and roll it back and forth for 5 minutes (longer for faster carbonation).
9. Rest, ideally overnight attached to the gas and kept cold (refrigerator, or a bucket packed with ice and water).
10. Remove the gas line just before dispensing.

**How to dispense:**
1. Disconnect the gas line; pull the release valve about 10 seconds until hissing is minimal.
2. Optionally swap in the standard lid.
3. Set the regulator to **5 PSI**, attach gas to the keg's own connector, vent once more for about 10 seconds.
4. Attach the tap with its liquid connector closed; draw a little, nudging PSI up in tiny steps if slow, down if foamy.
5. Keep the keg on ice while serving.
