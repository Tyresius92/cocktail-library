# Exploring Technique: Building Whisky Highballs (p214, condensed)

Three considerations plus a specific mixing technique:
1. **Proportion**: too little seltzer and the whisky tastes diluted with weak bubbles; too much dumbs down the flavor. Two parts seltzer to one part whisky makes the whisky the star, lifted on the effervescent backbone.
2. **Temperature**: smooth-interior glasses (etching gives gases more surface to escape), chilled glasses, and extremely cold seltzer, which also maintains bubbles.
3. **Ice**: many small pieces have a lot of surface area and make the drink go flat quickly; one large spear keeps bubbles but not cold. The authors choose 1-inch cubes.

## Method
Pull a chilled Highball glass from the freezer and pour in the whisky. Slowly lower 1 ice cube (preferably 1 inch or larger, straight from the freezer) into the whisky with a barspoon so it doesn't crack. Let it sit about 10 seconds (stirring immediately can crack the ice from the temperature difference). Slowly stir until condensation forms on the outside of the glass, about 10 seconds. Add another cube or two so the ice comes two-thirds of the way up, stir briefly. Pour in just enough chilled seltzer that the ice doesn't float. Insert a barspoon to the bottom and lift the lowest ice piece about an inch and lower it back, to gently mix whisky and seltzer. Add a final piece of ice and the remaining seltzer. One final revolution of stir only. Serve immediately.

(The short recipe cards in the chapter compress this to "add ice, stir 3 seconds, add seltzer, stir once".)
