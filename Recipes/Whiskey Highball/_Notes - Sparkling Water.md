# The Balance: Sparkling Water (p211, condensed)

The classic Highball is balanced solely by sparkling water, so its qualities and a judicious pour matter. Roles of sparkling water:
1. Dilutes the spirit, stretching its flavors and making it easier to drink; tight, complex flavors loosen up and become easier to identify.
2. Adds an impression of acidity: not like lemon or lime but a pleasant sharp tingle.
3. Enhances the spirit's aroma, lifting volatile fragrances to the drinker's nose.

More bubbles mean a greater effect, so the authors watch how carbonated the water is, and keep it well chilled (carbon dioxide leaves warm liquid faster).

- **Sparkling mineral water**: natural springs with dissolved minerals; some naturally effervescent, some carbonated; minerals, abundance and pH vary widely, so brands aren't interchangeable, though a subtle mineral flavor can be a delicious match with whisky.
- **Soda water / club soda**: still water with added minerals (sodium bicarbonate, sodium citrate, potassium sulfate) and carbonated: a subtle salty flavor that can brighten citrus and curb bitterness, like a bit of [[Salt Solution]] (p298). For more control the authors prefer seltzer and add salinity if needed.
- **[[Seltzer]]**: filtered carbonated water, neutral and consistent: preferred, and highly recommended for Highballs.

Highballs needn't be bubbly: they can use noncarbonated mixers, mainly juice. Old-style versions (Screwdriver, Salty Dog) have a bad reputation because of pasteurized, processed juice; with fresh ingredients they can be delicious. At the extreme end: the Bloody Mary.
