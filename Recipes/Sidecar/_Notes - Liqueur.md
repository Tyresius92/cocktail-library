# The Balance and Seasoning: Liqueur (pp. 172-177, condensed)

Liqueurs are alcohol, flavoring and sweetener. In cocktails, consider their sweetness and proof as much as flavor: both strongly affect balance. Bottles seldom indicate sugar content. Rough guide: fruit-focused liqueurs (cassis, framboise) are often quite sweet; citrus liqueurs like orange curacao can be shockingly dry. Proof must appear on the label; liqueurs are typically at least 20% ABV to be shelf stable, up to green Chartreuse at 55%. Low-ABV liqueurs taste sweeter, higher-proof ones drier.

## Fruit liqueurs (p172)
Almost any fruit has been used. Big difference between quality liqueurs and bargain brands: seek out the good stuff.

### Orange liqueur (triple sec / curacao)
Cointreau and Grand Marnier are the most famous. Curacao isn't flavored only with orange peel; spices support the orange. Made on neutral spirit (Cointreau) or a richer brandy base (Grand Marnier). Generally dry, so exceptionally versatile. In the Sidecar it's a significant component, generally a full ounce. In lesser quantities it supports a fortified wine (Troubled Leisure, p284) or adds a pop of flavor in tiny measures (Jump in the Line, p262).
- [[Cointreau]], [[Grand Marnier]], [[Pierre Ferrand dry curacao]]

### Grapefruit liqueur (pamplemousse, p172-173)
A newcomer that has taken the sour category by storm; balanced sweet and tart; deepens the impression of fruitiness. Rarely more than 1/2 oz; often 1/2 oz liqueur + 1/2 oz simple syrup in a sour.
- [[Giffard Creme de Pamplemousse]]

### Berry liqueur (p173-174)
Deeply sweet and juicy; some, like cassis, have a tart astringency that can overpower. Use 1/4 to 1/2 oz.
- [[Clear Creek Raspberry Liqueur]], [[Gabriel Boudier Creme de Cassis de Dijon]], [[Giffard Creme de Fraise des Bois]]

### Stone fruit liqueur (p174)
Apricot and peach are the authors' favorites (apricot in Normandie Club Martini No. 2, p282).
- [[Giffard Abricot du Roussillon]], [[Massenez Creme de Peche]], [[Giffard Creme de Peche]]

### Pear liqueur (p174)
Adds sweetness and texture. [[Clear Creek Pear Liqueur]] (roasted; pairs with aged spirits and sherry), [[Mathilde Pear Liqueur]] (fresh pear; citrusy drinks). Used in [[Rational Thought]] as pear brandy.

### Maraschino liqueur (p174)
Secret weapon of many cult cocktails: Aviation (p278), Martinez (p86). From sour marasca cherries of coastal Croatia, made from fruit and crushed pits, so almond-like; a powerful distilled spirit similar to a sweetened pomace brandy; funky and complex.
- [[Luxardo maraschino liqueur]], [[Maraska Maraschino]]

## Floral and herbal liqueurs (p175)
- **Elderflower**: [[St-Germain]]
- **Violet**: fundamental in the Aviation; overused it tastes like soap or perfume. Creme de violette is sweet and low proof; Creme Yvette is higher proof and dosed with other flavors. [[Giffard Creme de Violette]]
- **Creme de menthe / menthe de pastille**: cooling and refreshing; vital to the Stinger (p13) and Grasshopper (p268). Green and clear are identical in flavor. [[Giffard Menthe-Pastille]], [[Tempus Fugit Creme de Menthe]]

## Herbal liqueurs (p176)
Combination of many powerful flavors that create a new flavor; originally medicinal, high in proof and not terribly pleasant alone, so use small quantities and consider more sweetener.
- [[Benedictine]], [[Chareau California Aloe Liqueur]], [[Chartreuse]], [[Green Chartreuse]], [[Galliano l'Autentico]], [[Strega]]

## Rich liqueurs (p176-177)
Covered more fully in the Flip chapter (ch. 6) but discussed here. Often maligned for sweetness, but versatile; in moderation they season without overpowering.
- **Creme de cacao** (p177): bar staple thanks to the Brandy Alexander (p257), but also used nuancedly in the [[20th Century]]. Light and dark versions; dark is often caramel-colored, so use white. [[Giffard white creme de cacao]] (not more than 3/4 oz).
- **Vanilla liqueur**: vanilla can dumb down other flavors and these are quite sweet, so use with restraint, especially with oak-aged spirits. [[Giffard Vanille de Madagascar]]
- **Coffee liqueur**: many lack the complexity of fresh coffee; look for artisanal local ones. [[Caffe Lolita Licor de Cafe]], [[Galliano Ristretto]]
- Cream liqueurs: mentioned but not detailed on this page.
