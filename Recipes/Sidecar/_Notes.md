The defining traits of the Sidecar and its extended family of cocktails ("Sidecar Orthodoxy", p154):
- A Sidecar's core flavor is composed of a spirit and a substantial amount of flavorful liqueur.
- A Sidecar is both balanced and seasoned by liqueur, which also provides sweetness, sometimes in combination with another sweetener.
- A Sidecar is also balanced by highly acidic citrus juice, typically lemon or lime.

Root recipes: [[Sidecar]] (classic, p151), [[Ideal Sidecar]] (the book's version, p152).

## Liqueurs Come to the Party (p153, condensed)
The Daiquiri is a boisterous guest, the guy who knows everyone; the Sidecar is an exotic, quiet foreigner. Only three ingredients, yet mysterious: a punch of bright citrus, then bracing dryness, then a finish that hangs around, and it evolves as it warms from crisp and dry to rich and full. Sidecars are related to Daiquiris (both evolved from the sour template) but contain a significant amount of flavorful liqueur, generally 1/2 oz to 1 oz. Where Daiquiris are defined by rich texture and bright acidity, Sidecars are drier and gain complexity from the combination of liqueur and citrus. Because liqueurs vary in sweetness, proof and tannins, Sidecars are trickier to master. The same balance of strong, sweet and sour is sought, but since liqueur brings proof, sweetness and seasoning, the proportions differ and there is no standard template: study the liqueur and tweak accordingly.

## Understanding the Template (p154)
Same template as the Margarita (tequila, curacao, lime) and the Cosmopolitan (vodka, curacao, lime, cranberry). The Sidecar represents the style because its balance is hard to achieve with three ingredients that vary so much by producer: Cognac (light and tannic to rich and luscious), orange curacao (bone-dry like Pierre Ferrand, sweet like Grand Marnier, in between like Cointreau) and lemon juice.
