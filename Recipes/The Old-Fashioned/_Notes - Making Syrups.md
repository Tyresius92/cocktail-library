# Making Syrups (Next-Level Technique)

Condensed from pp. 42-57. A basic syrup is the most consistent, accurate way to add sweetness (granules vary too much sugar to sugar and bartender to bartender), and a great platform to build on: add flavors, thicken with gum arabic, change the sugar:water ratio, or swap in honey, agave or maple. Use filtered water. **Weigh, don't measure by volume**: a gram scale for large amounts (up to 4 kg) and a small one accurate to 0.01 g.

## Methods
- **Hand mixed** (simple, honey syrups): whisk or shake sweetener and water until dissolved; refrigerate. Used when ingredients integrate at room temperature and to keep the exact ratio (boiling evaporates water and eventually converts sucrose to sweeter glucose + fructose).
- **Blender** (strawberry, cane sugar, grenadine, ginger): blend on high until sugar dissolves and solids liquify, strain through a fine-mesh sieve if solids were used. Speeds up dissolving sugar and powders (citric acid) and avoids heat for delicate fruit.
- **Immersion circulator** (raspberry, cinnamon, cordial, gum syrups): combine by weight, seal in a heatproof resealable bag (dip the bag in water to push the air out before finishing the seal), cook in a water bath at the set temperature and time, cool in an ice bath, strain through a fine-mesh sieve and then a paper coffee filter or Superbag if particles remain. Temperatures used: 135F for delicate fruit and cordial, 145F for cinnamon and gum syrups, all for 2 hours. Also dissolves gum arabic in hours instead of days.
- **Direct heat** (spiced almond syrup only): rarely used because stoves vary and water evaporates, but heat extracts flavor faster. Simmer stirring frequently, then take off heat and cover so condensing water isn't lost, cool, strain.
- **Centrifuge** (clarified strawberry): optionally treat the syrup with Pectinex Ultra SP-L (0.2% of the syrup's weight, rest 15 minutes), spin at 4,500 rpm for 12 minutes with every container balanced to the same weight using water, then carefully strain off the solids. For translucent drinks or carbonation. No centrifuge: let the syrup sit overnight in the fridge and strain through at least four layers of cheesecloth.

## Special ingredients
- [[Gum Arabic]]: acacia sap; emulsifier and thickener, binds ingredients ([[Demerara Gum Syrup]], [[Pineapple Gum Syrup]]).
- [[Citric Acid]]: flavoring for syrups; a 5:1 water:citric acid solution can replace citrus juice when you want acidity without particles (important for carbonation).
- Lactic acid: tiny amounts add round texture (Vanilla Lactic Syrup, p. 286).
- [[Malic Acid]]: tartness, like green apples ([[House Grenadine]]).
- [[Pectinex Ultra SP-L]]: enzyme that breaks down pectin to clarify syrups.
- Superbag: reusable fine-mesh strainer bag (Modernist Pantry); 250 micron recommended for syrups.

## Shelf life and scaling
- Sugar preserves, but fruit syrups spoil faster; as a rule don't use syrups more than 2 weeks old (plain sweetener + water ones last longer). Taste before using; fizziness means fermenting, so discard.
- Recipes yield about 16 oz. Sugar and water scale simply; spices and extracts get too intense when scaled more than double, so start at two-thirds and adjust.
