# Mint Julep
## Root
[[Old Fashioned]]

## Ingredients
- 1 [[Mint]] bouquet #seasoning
- 2 oz [[Buffalo Trace bourbon]] #core
- 1/4 oz [[Simple Syrup]] #balance

## Method
Rub the interior of a Julep tin with the mint bouquet, then set the mint aside. Add the bourbon and syrup and fill the tin halfway with crushed ice. Holding the tin by the rim, stir, churning the ice as you go, for about 10 seconds. Add more crushed ice to fill about two-thirds full and stir until the tin is completely frosted. Add more ice to form a cone above the rim. Garnish with the mint bouquet and serve with a straw.

**Technique (condensed, p. 30):** the bouquet is 4-5 healthy mint sprigs, about 4 inches across, stems trimmed to 6 inches with the lower leaves removed. Hold the tin by the edge (skin oils prevent frosting). Insert the bouquet upside down and rub the leaves lightly on the inside of the tin, enough to smell mint without bruising. At the end, jiggle a straw into the ice to make a channel and slide the bouquet in so the sprigs look like they're growing from the ice.

## Notes
Classic. In essence a Julep is an especially refreshing Old-Fashioned that substitutes mint for the bitters (Southerners may object). Mint can be incorporated by muddling, steeping, or as an aromatic garnish. The book encourages playing with the template using other herbs (pineapple or chocolate mint, basil varieties, sage, even thyme), but some muddle badly (certain sages taste off-putting, muddled thyme is a mess), so use them as an aromatic garnish or extract their flavor via an infusion or syrup.
