# Understanding the Template (p244, condensed)

A Flip balances the core's personality, the sugar's sweetness and the egg's fat and froth. Egg is a fixed contribution and the sweetener amount is mostly personal preference, so the base ingredient is the main thing to consider.

- **Light, unaged spirits** (vodka, gin, unaged rum): the Flip comes out overwhelmingly boozy and out of balance, since proof rather than flavor is front and center.
- **Unaged spirits with strong personalities** (mezcal, tequila, eau-de-vie): may have enough body to mingle with the egg, but usually need tempering with a rich fortified wine such as sweet sherry or port.
- **Aged spirits** (brandy, whiskey) are a much better fit: vanilla and spice notes make a Flip round and full.
- **Amount**: 2 full oz of strong spirit still tends to make a noticeably boozy Flip, so the authors often cut to 1 1/2 oz of high-proof alcohol or use a low-ABV ingredient.
- **Fortified wines** are highly flavorful and have enough proof to assert themselves without being boozy, so they're great in Flips. Pick ones with rich body from years in oak or added sweetness; a light fino lacks the robustness. The perfect choice: amontillado, palo cortado and oloroso, whose nutty, fruity flavors bloom with the egg's fat and a bit extra sweetener. Nutmeg on top draws out the sherry's nut and spice.
