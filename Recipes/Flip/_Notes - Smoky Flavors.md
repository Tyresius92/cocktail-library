# Next-Level Technique: Introducing Smoky Flavors (pp274-276, condensed)

**Why in the Flip chapter:** the fat in egg and dairy traps the many components of smoke, so Flips carry smoke better than lean drinks (an Old-Fashioned variation can taste like liquid smoke). Keep the cocktail in mind and be judicious: too much dominates, too little does nothing, and tolerance varies.

**Basic ways:** smoky ingredients (paprika, cumin, chipotle) in syrups, infusions or rims; or real smoke: heat wood until smoldering and let the smoke meet the liquid's surface; it infuses in minutes. Infuse ingredients with a modest amount of smoke and pair with complementary flavors (vanilla from oak-aged spirits, barbecue sweetness, cigar tobacco).

## Tools (p276)
- **Smoking gun** (e.g. PolySience Smoking Gun Pro): fan draws smoke from the burn chamber through a tube submerged in liquid or aimed above a drink; cold smoke, adjustable output.
- **Wood stave or plank** (e.g. old bourbon barrel stave): torch the inner side until embers glow, set a glass on top to season it with vanilla-and-spice smoke; great for a dramatic Sazerac-style drink.
- **Butane torch**: lights chips, ignites herbs for a smoky garnish, chars staves.
- **Grill**: cook fruit or vegetables for syrups or mixes ([[Grilled Pineapple Syrup]], p285); on a gas grill use wood chips in a foil pouch.

## Smoking with wood (p276)
Use hardwood only (soft woods like cedar, pine, fir, spruce and hemlock make harsh, unsafe smoke). Least to most smoky:
- **Mild** (cherry, apple, peach, pear, birch): sweet and fruity; light ingredients like fresh fruit in syrups.
- **Medium** (oak, hickory, maple, pecan, sarsaparilla): if you pick one, oak or hickory; oak suits any aged spirit, hickory suits American whiskey or apple brandy.
- **Strong** (mesquite, pimento, walnut): overpowers; use to season a glass or the top of a drink.

**Smoking gun method:** pack small shavings in an even, not-too-tight layer, light with a torch, let the first whiffs and flame subside; when it is smoldering with red embers insert the tube into the liquid (or above it in a covered container) and smoke for about ten seconds; use a container large enough to trap smoke when sealed and let it linger until it clears.

## Smoking with flammable ingredients (p277)
- **Tea leaves** in a smoking gun: pick naturally smoky teas (Lapsang souchong) or woodsy-fruity blends like August Uncommon Tea's A Field in Innsbruck; experiment, some act like soft wood and give sweet smoke.
- **Herbs and spices**: torch a long rosemary sprig (or cinnamon) so the aroma perfumes the drink or room; set it on the drink carefully (embers).
- **Smoke-infused ice**: ice absorbs aromas (hence dedicated ice freezers); put ice in a lidded plastic container, inject dense smoke with the gun tube, seal, freeze a few hours. Subtle smoke for Old-Fashioned-style drinks.
