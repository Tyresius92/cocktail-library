# Exploring Technique: Using Eggs in Cocktails (p264, condensed)

- Never put liquor or citrus on top of the egg: acid and alcohol "cook" it if left standing, giving an off texture and flavor. Crack the egg (or the separated white) into the large tin first; build the rest of the drink in the small tin, keeping it apart from the egg. (Also easier to fish out shell.)
- **Dry shake first** (no ice; ice inhibits foaming): seal the tin tightly, since expanding proteins build pressure, and shake hard about 5 seconds.
- Open and check for foam, transfer the foamy liquid to the small tin, fill with ice, seal and shake vigorously, then a few seconds longer: dilutes, chills and traps air in the egg protein for a stronger, more consistent foam.
- **Double strain** every egg cocktail to remove ice chips and shell bits; the mesh also aerates.
- Cleaning: don't dunk egg-y tools in a sink of water. Rinse the shaker and sieve individually and thoroughly, then smell them; any hint of egg, wash again.
- **Reverse dry shake** (shake with ice first, strain, then shake again without ice): proponents claim a bigger, longer-lasting head; the authors found the difference marginal and prefer the regular dry shake as fewer steps.
