# Friends and Family: Trevor Easter (p263, condensed)

Trevor Easter was general manager of the Walker Inn and the Normandie Club (2016-2017); earlier at Bourbon & Branch and Rickhouse in San Francisco and Noble Experiment in San Diego.

Gist of his essay (paraphrased):
- His first Flip was at 15 Romolo in San Francisco; cracking a whole egg at the bar draws curiosity, and curiosity sells the drink.
- Flips are messy and slow service, so he keeps them off the menu but offers them as a bartender's choice when a guest wants something decadent.
- Stigma: richness gets equated with sweetness, but a balanced Flip isn't sweet. It sits between cocktail and custard, perfect after a meal instead of dessert.
- His approach: aged spirit base (woodiness), often with sherry or port, then play with the sweetener (maple, honey).
- Tips: let the egg warm up a minute out of the fridge for a bigger frothy head; the "David and Goliath shake": after the dry shake, add a large ice block plus a Kold-Draft cube to the tin, so the big block shatters the small one, chills fast, and acts like a piston to add air.
- Use leftover yolks from egg-white drinks in a Flip-style drink as a treat for a guest.
- He sees the Flip as an untouched frontier: most bartenders can riff on an Old-Fashioned or Daiquiri but hit a dead end with a Flip.
