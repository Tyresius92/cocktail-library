# Port (p250-251, condensed)

Fortified wine from Portugal's Douro Valley, a blend of several Portuguese grapes, typically red (white and rose exist). Fermentation is stopped around the halfway point by adding young grape brandy (to at least 17.5% ABV), preserving some sweetness.

## Styles
- **Ruby**: nonvintage, wood-aged at least 2 years.
- **Vintage**: 2+ years in barrel, then years in bottle (softens tannins, develops fruit).
- **Late-bottled vintage**: longer in barrel (4-6 years), meant to be drunk younger.
- **Tawny**: blend of multiple vintages, not meant for bottle aging.
- **Colheita**: single year, aged extensively in barrel, not for bottle aging.

## In cocktails
Robust, tannic and high in alcohol, so a good sweet-vermouth substitute in Manhattan-style drinks. It can be the core of any style of drink, but it's relatively sweet, so adjust the other ingredients. A young tawny adds depth while staying juicy (older ones add structure and pair with strong flavors like single-malt scotch, as in the Chancellor, p279); tawny plus sweet vermouth accentuates the vermouth's hidden juiciness. Young ruby is fresh and berry-forward and good with citrus but lacks the structure for spirituous drinks, where you need a vintage or late-bottled vintage.
