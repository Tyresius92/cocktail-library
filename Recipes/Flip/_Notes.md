# The Flip (Chapter 6, pp. 241-)

Root recipes: [[Flip]] (classic, p241) and [[Ideal Flip]] (the book's version, p242).

## Flip Orthodoxy (p243)
- A Flip's characteristic flavor comes from a core spirit or fortified wine combined with a rich ingredient.
- A Flip is balanced by its rich ingredients: eggs, dairy, coconut milk, or dense liqueurs and syrups.
- A Flip is seasoned with aromatic spices on top of the finished cocktail, a role that can also be played by a highly flavorful liqueur such as amaro.

## Strike It Rich (p243, condensed)
Nowhere else in cocktails is decadence celebrated as much: dessert in a glass, often made with creamy ingredients and flavors (chocolate, mint, coffee) that fancy cocktail bars long derided. The Mudslide, White Russian, Grasshopper and Brandy Alexander all stem from the basic Flip, and are as worthy of study as any craft cocktail. Simple: a slug of sherry (or other spirit), sweetener and an egg, shaken and dusted with nutmeg. Add heavy cream and it becomes a nog. A balanced Flip puts the core spirit center stage, with egg, sugar and sometimes cream in supporting roles. Where the Old-Fashioned and Martini are contemplative and the Daiquiri, Sidecar and Highball invigorating, the Flip is all comfort.

Riffing: start with the core's flavor, strength and sweetness, then balance with sugar, egg, cream or other ingredients. Jerry Thomas's 1862 *Bar-Tender's Guide* set the cold brandy Flip as the standard, then gave hot and cold variations with rum, gin, whiskey, port, sherry and ale: an early version of this book's approach.
