# Exploring Technique: Shaking to Dilution (pp. 124-126)

Goal of any cocktail that's shaken and served up ("shaking to dilution" or full-dilution shaking): a drink that's cold, properly diluted and well aerated. For the Daiquiri, a frothy drink with a noticeable layer of white foam on top. Related: [[_Notes - Full-Dilution Stirring]] (the stirred counterpart).

Shaker: the authors prefer a pair of weighted stainless tins ("tin on tin"), one small (18 oz) and one large (28 oz); a Boston or cobbler shaker works too. The variable discussed is the ice. Three styles:

| Ice | Shake time |
|---|---|
| One large cube (about 2 1/2 in) | 15 seconds |
| 1-inch cubes | 10 seconds |
| "Shitty" ice | 5 seconds |

## One large ice cube (preferred)
Aerates well without making tiny ice chips, so no double straining is needed, and makes a consistent style and duration easier because the ice isn't a variable.
1. Build the drink in the small tin (cheapest ingredients first, syrup and juice, in case you botch it). Building before adding ice controls when dilution begins.
2. Slide the block of ice in, place the large tin over the small at an angle, hit the top with your palm to seal.
3. Hold with the small tin's top facing your body (so you're in harm's way if it separates). Grip at the ends with minimal palm contact; palms warm the tins.
4. Give it a few slow turns to temper the ice so it doesn't shatter.
5. Shake in front of your chest, a push-pull motion along a gentle arc so the ice moves in an ellipse (rounding the edges rather than shattering). Start slow, speed up to an intensity you can hold for about 10 seconds, then slow down over about as long as you took to speed up (slow-fast-slow).
6. Set the shaker down with the large tin on the bottom, squeeze the sides of the large tin while pushing the small tin away to break the seal. Strain through a Hawthorne strainer right away.
Feedback: the chunk-chunk of the block gets muted as it rounds off, the volume of liquid rises and the shaker gets colder. It takes many cocktails to learn when it's ready.

## 1-inch ice cubes
Ideally commercial-machine ice (e.g. Kold-Draft); freezer-tray ice has trapped gases and impurities that make it shatter and dilute faster. Build in the small tin, add enough ice that the liquid nearly reaches the top, seal, roll a few times to temper. Same motion and rhythm, shorter shake (more ice surface area dilutes faster). Double strain (Hawthorne plus fine-mesh sieve) to catch ice chips that ruin Daiquiri texture.

## Shitty ice
Hotel-hallway ice, freezer-maker ice or small tray cubes: small, wet and impure, so it dilutes fastest; counterintuitively, use more ice. Fill the small tin with ice until the liquid nearly reaches the top, add ice to the large tin until it's about a quarter full (a cobbler shaker can be filled to the rim). Seal, no tempering; work quickly to avoid overdilution. The shake is even shorter, you'll hear little but sloshing, and the ice often fuses into one mass. Double strain.

## Gauging your technique
Look at the wash line (liquid level below the rim) after straining, and at the top. A Daiquiri should have a layer of white foam that lasts 30 seconds or longer. No foam: not shaking hard or long enough during the most intense stage (a long, slow shake never yields foam). A short vigorous shake may foam, but the wash line won't be as high, which means it didn't reach full dilution.
