# Daiquiri: Experimenting with the Seasoning

Condensed from pp. 122. In the Martini, aroma seasons the drink via a twist, olive or onion. In the Daiquiri the lime wedge does the same job, as do wedges, wheels and half wheels in other cocktails.

- A wedge can be squeezed in to add acidity (so drinkers can customize) and releases citrus oils (aromatic seasoning). Even untouched on the rim it gives a noticeable hit of bright citrus aroma.
- A wheel or half wheel dropped into a drink (as in the Negroni, see [[Negroni]]) slowly flavors it as it's consumed: juice infuses and alcohol draws out citrus oils. As with a lemon twist left too long in a Martini, it can turn bitter. Remove a wheel when you're about half done with the drink.

## The experiment
Make two Daiquiris with your favorite recipe from this chapter. In the first, leave the wedge on the rim. In the second, squeeze the lime wedge in and drop the spent wedge into the drink.
- Sip both: the second is noticeably tarter, with a different aroma from the released oils (the first has a similar, fainter aroma from the wedge on the rim).
- After 2 minutes the second tastes more bitter, because the alcohol is drawing bitter compounds out of the pith.
- After 5 minutes the first is still balanced and refreshing (a little warm); the second is distinctly bitter and tart, unpleasant qualities emphasized as it warms.

Takeaway: a seemingly innocuous garnish can push a cocktail out of balance. The authors love a tart drink, so they squeeze the wedge in and then discard it.
