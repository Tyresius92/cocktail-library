# Next-Level Technique: Clarifying (pp. 144-147, condensed)

Clarification removes cloudy particles from juice, giving the flavor and balance of fresh juice without the color and density. Inspired by Dave Arnold's *Liquid Intelligence* and the *Cooking Issues* blog. Why bother: playing with expectations (a crystal-clear drink that looks like a Martini but tastes bright and acidic), and fully carbonated cocktails (end of chapter 5), where removing particulates lets CO2 disperse evenly. Clarified juice is more fragile than fresh: use within one day, unless mixed into batched or kegged cocktails with sweeteners and spirits (p236).

The authors prefer a mechanical centrifuge or agar over a paper filter; they clarify citrus, fruit and vegetable juices and purees.

## The experiment (p145)
Make the root [[Ideal Daiquiri]] and the [[Clarified Daiquiri]] (clarified lime juice, stirred). Clarified juice is a world apart: lighter and less penetrating, so rum is more pronounced. Clarification can also reset biases: the authors shy away from orange juice because its flavor varies widely and it homogenizes drinks. The [[Monkey Gland (Classic)]] is the example; [[Monkey Gland (Clarified Orange Juice)]] is the fix.

## How to clarify juice with agar (p146)
Gear: juicer, fine-mesh sieve lined with cheesecloth, bowls, gram scale, saucepan, agar, whisk, Superbag or paper coffee filter.
1. Juice and strain through a fine-mesh sieve; prepare an ice bath.
2. Weigh the juice.
3. Weigh out water equal to 25% of the juice weight.
4. Add juice and water weights, take 0.2% of that total: the grams of agar.
5. Cook the water and agar in a saucepan over medium heat, whisking constantly, until dissolved.
6. Off the heat, whisk in the juice.
7. Pour into a container set in the ice bath.
8. After about 10 minutes it sets; whisk into curd-like chunks.
9. Transfer to a cheesecloth-lined sieve over a bowl.
10. Gather the cheesecloth into a ball and squeeze out the liquid gently (too hard and agar comes through).
11. If particles remain, pass through a Superbag or paper coffee filter.
12. Refrigerate until ready to use.

## How to clarify juice with a centrifuge (p147)
Gear: juicer, sieve and cheesecloth, gram scale, centrifuge containers with lids, [[Pectinex Ultra SP-L]], [[Kieselsol]], [[Chitosan]], centrifuge, Superbag.
1. Juice and strain.
2. Weigh the juice in a centrifuge container.
3. X = 0.2% of the juice weight.
4. Stir X grams of Pectinex Ultra SP-L and kieselsol into the juice (as printed; unclear whether X grams of each). Cover, rest 15 minutes.
5. Stir in X grams of chitosan. Cover, rest 15 minutes.
6. Stir in X grams of kieselsol right before centrifuging.
7. Balance the other containers with equal weights of water.
8. Run at 4,500 rpm for 12 minutes.
9. Carefully strain through cheesecloth or a Superbag without disturbing the solids.
10. Refrigerate until ready to use.

## Special ingredients (p147)
- [[Kieselsol]] and [[Chitosan]]: wine-industry fining agents sold as a set. Suspended solids carry a charge; kieselsol (negative, added first) and chitosan (positive, added second) bind particles of the opposite charge into heavier clumps, which the centrifuge then separates quickly (important since cocktail ingredients deteriorate fast).
- [[Agar]]: the gel alternative, derived from algae and vegan-friendly (egg whites or gelatin also work). Kieselsol and chitosan don't work as well without a centrifuge, so use agar when you don't have one.
- Pectinex Ultra SP-L: see p48 of the book.
