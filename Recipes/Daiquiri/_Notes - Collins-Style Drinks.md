# Sours with Bubbles: Collins-Style Drinks (pp. 138-139)

The other branch of the extended family: sours with bubbly ingredients, in subfamilies of Collins-style drinks, fizzes, and cocktails with sparkling wine. The Collins is basically a sour served in a tall glass with ice and seltzer.

- Biggest challenge: avoiding overdilution. The authors want them as fizzy as possible, so they short shake (about 5 seconds, just long enough to chill) rather than shake to full dilution as for sours.
- Add a measured portion of seltzer to the empty serving glass first, pour in the shaker contents (which mixes it, no stirring), and add ice at the very end. The traditional top-with-unstirred-seltzer approach tends to drink like a sour that gradually dilutes and may not be bubbly at all.
- Seltzer should be as cold as possible: keep it refrigerated and sealed, or build a carbonating rig (p232) for very cold, highly sparkling seltzer. Room-temperature seltzer just dilutes the drink.
- Seltzer's carbonic acid increases perceived acidity, so use roughly 1:1 citrus to sweetener unless you want an extra-tart drink.
