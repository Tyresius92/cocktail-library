# The Core: Rum

Condensed from p. 107. Rum is "downright resistant to rules": styles vary by origin, aging and distillation. All rums are made from sugarcane (molasses as an industrial by-product of sugar refining, or fresh cane juice). Rum is still unofficially classified by colonial border: Spanish, English, French, plus Jamaican, but modern rums blur those lines, and many producers blend across locales and styles. Variation from producer to producer is much greater than with most strong spirits.

- A rum's personality is largely set by the still: **column** still rums are bright and fresh even after oak aging; **pot** still rums keep more flavor and a richer texture (yielding a lower-proof spirit). Taste blind to learn the difference: focused and light is column, big and funky is pot.
- Light rums are often aged and then filtered heavily to remove color (and some aged flavors). Gold rums are lumped into the light category (a bit more oak, similar performance). Dark rums are aged and also colored (to deepen hue and sometimes flavor). Aged rums get their color mostly from oak.
- **Rum, rhum, ron:** *ron* in Spanish-speaking islands; *rhum* (French) for rums from fresh sugarcane distillate in former French colonies, Guadeloupe and Martinique; plain *rum* on former British islands, made from molasses.

## Styles and bottles (pp. 108-112, condensed)
- **Spanish-style** (Puerto Rico, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Venezuela, Guatemala, Nicaragua, Panama, Colombia, Costa Rica, Ecuador): from nineteenth-century distillers who used column distillation to make high-proof rums stripped of pot-still aggression, mostly heavily filtered. Smooth, rounded, vanilla undertones: a lovely companion for lime in a Daiquiri. Light rums go in citrusy cocktails like the Daiquiri; dark rums in Dark and Stormys and complex tiki drinks; aged rums in a decadent Daiquiri or spirituous drinks (aged Spanish-style rum pairs particularly well with Cognac, as in Thick as Thieves, p284).
  - Light: [[Cana Brava white rum]], [[Flor de Cana 4-Year White]], [[Plantation 3 Stars White]]
  - Dark/aged: [[Gosling's Black Seal]], [[Ron del Barrilito Three Stars]], [[Zacapa 23]]
- **English-style** (former British Caribbean colonies): mostly molasses, like Spanish-style, but richer and funkier from blending light-, medium- and full-bodied rums from different stills.
  - [[El Dorado White Rum]], [[Cruzan Black Strap]], [[Plantation Old-Fashioned Traditional Dark Overproof]], [[El Dorado 15-Year]]
- **Jamaican-style**: always pot-stilled: richness and an intense grassy aroma; "a distinctive, profound funkiness". Rarely the sole base; combined with other spirits or fortified wine (Fair Game, p280; [[Last One Standing]]).
  - [[Wray & Nephew White Overproof]], [[Hamilton Jamaican pot still gold rum]], [[Smith & Cross]], [[Hamilton Jamaican Pot Still Dark]], [[Appleton Estate Reserve Blend]]
- **French-style rhum agricole** (Martinique, Guadeloupe, Marie-Galante, Haiti): from fresh sugarcane juice (a Napoleonic Wars-era shift), with aging categories borrowed from Cognac and often copper pot stills. Grit and personality that sharpen citrus with a bright tropical tang (molasses rums keep citrus front and center; agricole adds tang). Also used as a modifier alongside another base (another lighter rum for complexity, tequila, gin, Cognac) or to boost flavor in low-ABV sherry and fortified-rum drinks.
  - [[Barbancourt White]], [[La Favorite Coeur de Canne rhum agricole blanc]], [[Rhum JM VSOP]], [[Neisson Reserve Speciale]]
- **Cachaca**: from Brazilian sugarcane juice, distilled only once to 38-48% ABV (rhum agricole is generally distilled twice), so richer and softer. White (branca/classica/tradicional/prata) is like a light rhum agricole: grassy and complex with banana and dried stone fruit; pair with lime; use smaller amounts. Aged cachacas use indigenous Brazilian woods, giving unique flavors.
  - [[Avua Prata]], [[Novo Fogo Silver]], [[Avua Amburana cachaca]]
