# Technique: Full-Dilution Stirring

Condensed from pp. 81-82. Martinis and almost all stirred drinks served neat (no ice in the glass) are stirred to full dilution, unlike the Old-Fashioned, which is deliberately underdiluted (see [[_Notes]] of the Old-Fashioned).

1. Use a mixing glass fresh from the freezer (a chilled glass slows dilution) and 1-inch ice cubes. Build the drink without ice, then fill the glass nearly full with ice (the ice on top presses the rest into the liquid). Cracking about a third of the cubes speeds chilling.
2. One stir, then taste: disjointed, each component distinguishable.
3. Stir 10 seconds, taste: more integrated but still "tight" (around 40F, chilled but not cold).
4. Stir 10 more seconds, taste: cohesive like a chord, bracingly cold, just below the freezing point of water.
5. Keep going until it tastes weaker. When water gets in the way of flavor, you've gone past the sweet spot.
