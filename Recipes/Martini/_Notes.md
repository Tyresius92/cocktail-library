The defining traits of the Martini and its extended family (p. 64, "Martini Orthodoxy"):
- A Martini is composed of alcohol and aromatized wine, typically gin or vodka and dry vermouth.
- A Martini is flexible in regard to the proportions of those ingredients; its balance depends on the preference of the drinker.
- A Martini's garnish has a big impact on the overall flavor and experience of the drink.

Root recipes: [[Gin Martini]] and [[Vodka Martini]] (the standard recipes, p61) and the book's versions [[Ideal Gin Martini]] and [[Ideal Vodka Martini]] (p62).

## The template (p. 65, condensed)
- The core is a collaboration between gin or vodka and dry vermouth, not the spirit alone. With no sweetener, the vermouth must also balance the intensity of the base spirit, so the core lies in their interaction.
- The spirit brings proof and flavor; the aromatized wine adds flavor, acidity and sweetness and curbs the alcoholic intensity. The result is spirituous but smooth and soothing.
- Rule of thumb: the stronger the base spirit, the more vermouth needed. The root Gin Martini is "wet" (1 oz vermouth to 2 oz gin); the root Vodka Martini uses only 1/2 oz because vodka's nuance is overshadowed by too much vermouth.
- Once the core is balanced you can swap the base spirit (Manhattan, p84: rye and sweet vermouth, same ratios), tinker with the balance and the aromatized wine (Vesper, p71, uses Lillet blanc), or add flavors that accent the core and balance (amari as in the Negroni, p89; small amounts of liqueur as in the Martinez, p86).
- Why the Martini rather than the Manhattan: less to hide behind, so small shifts in proportion have a bigger effect.

## Other notes
- Preference is personal ("poet or banker?"): gin for traditionalists, vodka for rebels, lemon twist and extra vermouth for poets, extra dry with olives for bankers. The garnish brings lemon oil brightness, olive salinity, a pickled onion's savor, or nothing.
- The book prefers stirred (shaking is possible, but it'll try to talk you out of it). Taste has moved from big aggressive flavors toward the smallest, most delicate details.
- Spirits: [[Gin]] and [[Vodka]].

- [[_Notes - Aromatized Wines]]: vermouth and other aromatized wines, with recommended bottles
- Core strategy (p70): swap the base spirit ("Mr. Potato Head"), but keep the relationship between spirit and aromatized wine intact; any change to the base will probably demand a change to the vermouth. Don't push the template past balance. See [[_Notes - Martini - Experimenting with the Core]].
- Balance (p75): the Martini's core is a base spirit plus something else (vermouth, another aromatized wine, a fortified wine like fino sherry, or a combination such as the Bamboo, p91). Changing either the base or the vermouth changes proof, sweetness and flavor, so rebalance. Experiment: three Martinis with different amounts of vermouth (bitters and garnish removed), made with fresh vermouth. See [[_Notes - Martini - Experimenting with the Balance]].
